Cravory

Restaurant Website Redesign

Design Rationale

This redesign of Cravory's website focuses on improving usability, visual appeal, and content presentation. Through my analysis of the original website, I identified several issues, including a lack of food imagery, inefficient use of space, inconsistent visual design, and a menu page that was difficult to browse.

To address these problems, I redesigned the landing page to feature high-quality food photography that better aligns with users' expectations when visiting a restaurant website. I also reorganized content to make better use of available space while maintaining readability and visual balance. The design was created with responsiveness in mind, ensuring a seamless experience across desktop and mobile devices without requiring horizontal scrolling.

The visual identity was refreshed through the use of green and off-white tones to communicate freshness, quality, and cleanliness while reducing the empty feeling created by large white spaces in the original design. The menu page received the most significant redesign, incorporating consistent typography, improved organization, and stronger visual hierarchy. Finally, I introduced an About page to preserve and highlight the restaurant's story, values, and emphasis on fresh ingredients and culinary expertise.

Overall, the redesign creates a more engaging and user-friendly experience that better showcases Cravory's offerings and encourages customers to make reservations.

Smirnoff Berry Blast

Beverage Redesign

Design Rationale

This project reimagines Smirnoff Berry Blast as Smirnoff Sweet Stuff, a flavored vodka brand that fully embraces the product's defining characteristic: its sweetness. Rather than competing within traditional liquor branding, which often emphasizes heritage, craftsmanship, and sophistication, the redesign intentionally moves in the opposite direction. The new identity targets consumers aged 19–25, including university students and new vodka drinkers who prioritize flavor, nightlife, and social experiences over connoisseurship. The goal was to create a brand that is self-aware, playful, and unapologetically indulgent.

The visual identity was built around the concept of confidence through self-expression. Bright blue packaging, nostalgic popsicle imagery, and a custom "Sweet Stuff" wordmark create a bold shelf presence while reinforcing the brand's sweet and energetic personality. The redesign intentionally distances itself from luxury liquor aesthetics, instead leaning into vibrant colors, humor, and nightlife culture. This direction is supported by the tagline "It's so good though," which acknowledges criticism from traditional vodka enthusiasts while confidently embracing the product's appeal. Together, the visual language and messaging communicate a clear brand promise: enjoy what you enjoy, even if it stands out.

To support the rebrand, I developed a social media strategy focused on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Content centers around humorous, highly shareable scenarios in which characters experience exaggerated appearance changes after drinking Sweet Stuff, symbolizing the confidence to stand out. Across the campaign, individuals transform from ordinary or overly serious versions of themselves into bold, colorful personalities, often wearing the signature pink suit featured throughout the brand. This concept culminates in the "It's Too Good" campaign, where every advertisement ends with the phrase "It's too good to not notice." These visual transformations act as a metaphor for the product itself: a sweet, eye-catching vodka that proudly rejects convention and embraces being different.
